I N T R O D U C T I O N

A home workspace is a designated area in a house where remote workers and students perform their professional tasks, such as freelance work, online learning/training, and creative projects. At times, these activities may also involve electrical systems and tools as part of their day-to-day activities.

Fire hazards are inevitable. According to DOLE in 2024, the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) reported that fire incidents increased by 25% compared to the previous year. Electricity was identified as the leading cause of the total incidents—specifically, octopus connections, DIY repairs, and the use of substandard electrical components.

This is why individuals must have prior knowledge about how to work with electrical systems and master each electrical tools’ purpose and right use. In doing so, they can create a much safer home working environment and prevent potential hazards.

01

The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety (CCOHS) states that powered hand tools should be inspected for damage before using them. Examining power cords for fraying, cracking, or worn insulation, looking for defects in switches and plugs, and checking the handle and body casing for cracks or other damage should all be part of the inspection. Checking batteries for corrosion, swelling, leakage, and cracks is also important. These inspections can help identify damaged parts that could make the tool dangerous to use.

02

Using the correct tool for a specific task is an important safety practice in Industrial Arts. Tools should be used according to their intended purpose and proper operating procedures. Using a tool incorrectly can cause damage to the equipment or result in accidents and injuries. Users should also make sure they understand how a tool works before operating it. Basic safety training can help people learn the proper and safe use of different tools.

03

Personal protective equipment (PPE) can help protect users from common hazards while working with tools. Safety goggles, gloves, masks, and other protective equipment may be needed depending on the task being performed. Wearing the appropriate PPE can reduce exposure to hazards such as flying particles, dust, sharp materials, and sparks. However, PPE should be used together with proper work practices and safe tool handling. Teaching users when and how to use PPE is an important part of basic safety training in Industrial Arts.
